## Two bounds for one original source expression

Let B0 be the fixed initial source-frame basis, L(t) the original local affine
linear transport, and c(t) its original translated origin. Both are freshly
bounded by the original MultiSweep owner. In normalized carrier coordinates,

    q = (1-t) C0(u,v) + t C1(u,v) - P(0)
    F = c(t) + L(t) B0 q.

The old evaluation encloses B0*q first and then multiplies by L. The additional
evaluation first encloses W=L*B0 and then multiplies by the original world
carrier jets. Both use outward interval products and sums of the SAME original
source expression. The latter retains correlation between carrier coordinates
that independent local-coordinate boxes lose for nonplanar filled carriers.
Neither a rounded composed matrix nor a transformed floating control net
becomes a replacement source.

The same identity applies to every first derivative:

    F_u = L B0 q_u
    F_v = L B0 q_v
    F_t = c' + L' B0 q + L B0 (C1-C0).

The final term is essential for different endpoint carriers. Both interval
evaluations enclose these full original derivatives. Componentwise intersection
therefore remains sound. If one evaluation suffers numeric overflow, only the
other independently valid enclosure may be retained. No invalid or empty
intersection becomes proof. Each source pose is paid once and cached only
inside its immutable source context; the extra fixed-size matrix arithmetic
performs no new source queries and supplies no borrowed-source authority.

## Weighted whole-matrix inverse proof

For a complete original convex parameter hull H, let J enclose EVERY one-sided
Jacobian on H. Positive diagonal S and finite matrices R,P are proposals only.
For each entry of B=I-R*P*J*S, outward arithmetic supplies M_ij >= |B_ij| for
ALL real matrices in that interval box. A positive finite vector d is proposed
from a floating solve, normalized, and independently checked by strict outward
row inequalities:

    sum_j M_ij d_j < d_i, for every i.

Thus ||I-R*P*A*S||_(infinity,d)<1 for every A in J. Neumann's theorem makes
R*P*A*S invertible, hence A is invertible. The rounded inverse, determinant,
weights and numerical solve provide NO admission credit without those final
inequalities. A large acyclic coupling may pass this weighted norm even when
a Frobenius perturbation bound fails. Singular matrices and focal cycles must
refuse. The former verified Frobenius route remains a fallback.

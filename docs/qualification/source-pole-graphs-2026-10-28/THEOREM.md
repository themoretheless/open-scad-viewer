# Conditional pole graph theorem used by the Rust factory

## Native premises

1. The borrowed private source body has embedded source faces, qualified pairwise contacts and one closed oriented vertex-link cycle. All incident faces at each selected pole are covered by the fresh plane/atlas proof.
2. Each incident face is a complete natural UV rectangle, with one original collapsed side. Its collar is globally injective under the selected two-coordinate projection modulo that side. For normalized inward coordinate u, the original projected radial distance is at least beta*u, beta>0. All nonpole collar projections therefore avoid the projected pole.
3. The same dropped world axis is transverse throughout every punctured collar at a pole. Source material winding and the body orientation give the same sign of the projected oriented area on every incident face. The source pole planes have one common exact oriented limit.
4. Natural meridians are full positive-weight rational-linear UV curves. The sealed canonical carrier proves exact original composition, strictly monotone parameter maps and common canonical endpoint identities. The natural pcurve derivative is nonzero. Both directed collar-cut transports have a strictly positive outward lower inward-coordinate bound in the other chart.
5. All chart rectangles, shared meridian uses and the actual owned edge-end link are complete. No public diagnostic flag is accepted in place of these facts.

## Compact star and its boundary

Let K be the union of the closed original collars, glued by their actual owned meridians. It is an embedded compact star with a disk neighborhood at the selected pole P. It need not have matching outer cut positions in neighboring source parameters.

Each meridian's cuts are prefixes from the same pole endpoint in one strictly monotone canonical parameter. Their intersection is the shorter prefix. Any unmatched tail belongs to the longer collar, and its inward coordinate there is at least the transported shorter cut. Multiplying that lower bound by the longer chart's beta bounds the tail's projected distance from p=projection(P). Taking the minimum over both directed transports is valid without guessing which cut is shorter. Outer radial arcs have their independent beta*u separation. Thus every part of the star boundary is at projected distance at least s>0. Outer corners or additional identifications at full chart ends lie on those excluded boundary parts.

The factory returns delta<s by outward multiplication by one half. It refuses a nonpositive or unresolved bound.

## One projected sheet

For nonpole interior points of K, projection is a local homeomorphism. In chart interiors this follows from the nonzero projected Jacobian. Along a shared meridian the projected curve is regular; exact common-edge composition identifies it, while opposite boundary traversal and the common oriented Jacobian sign place the two material interiors on opposite sides. No unmatched tail or outer cut lies over the open delta disk.

Let T be the part of K projected into that open disk. It contains a body neighborhood of P. Projection from T minus P to the punctured disk is a proper local homeomorphism: compact target sets avoid p, and their inverse images are closed in compact K, avoiding P and every star boundary. Its image is nonempty, open and closed, hence is the entire connected punctured disk. It is a finite covering.

Every covering component accumulates at P, because P is the only point in K projecting to p. A sufficiently small punctured body-disk neighborhood of P lies in T and is connected. Therefore the covering domain is connected.

An embedded surface in 3D gives a strict total order of the finitely many points over any projected point by their dropped world coordinate. The ordering cannot change under continuous continuation without two sheets meeting in 3D. It supplies global disjoint sections of the covering. Connectedness therefore forces covering degree one. Continuity and compactness at P extend this to a homeomorphism of T onto the open projected disk. The statement concerns this local body star; remote parts of the body may have overlapping projections.

## Differentiability at the pole

The exact original plane-limit proofs give a single limiting oriented normal, uniformly over all boundary approaches of every incident chart, with a nonzero dropped component. The graph is analytic on face interiors and extends analytically across a meridian within each source chart away from P. Its facewise gradients converge uniformly to the gradient determined by that common plane. Radial separation bounds each original inward coordinate by projected distance divided by beta, so convergence is uniform over the finite star.

Projected meridians are rational curves of bounded degree. A straight segment has finitely many intersections with them unless it coincides with an arc; on a coincident arc an adjacent chart gives the same height and directional derivative. Integrating the uniformly small gradient residual along a segment from p proves f(p+h)-f(p)-g*h=o(|h|). Thus the graph is differentiable at the pole. Ordinary seams away from P may still have jumps of gradient; this theorem makes no whole-neighborhood C1 or G2 assertion.

## Refusal and scope

Failure of any native premise or positive separation, unsupported non-natural retained boundaries, or exhaustion of exact work, original/projected cells, projection columns or boundary steps returns no pole graph atlas. Sharp ordinary joints remain C0. This theorem is about the unchanged original retained source body, not an unproved ideal loft/sweep law behind it.

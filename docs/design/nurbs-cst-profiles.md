# CST construction and inverse fitting

The model is `v(u) = u^n1 * (1-u)^n2 * sum a_i B_i(u)`, for normalized station `u` in [0,1]. `CstProfile` coefficients are dimensionless. This checkpoint counts two capabilities: a checked NURBS constructor (`cst_airfoil`) and inverse fitting with world placement (`cst_fit`, `cst_fit_in_frame`, `cst_fit_sides`). Input variants and frame helpers are not separate capabilities.

`cst_airfoil` uses checked analytic samples and the existing least-squares airfoil fitter. Degree 1..25, at most 64 controls and 4096 fit sites remain unchanged. Raw `cst_points` allows 8..2048 stations per side, but a 2048-station loop has 4097 sites and is refused by the NURBS fitter. Profile exponents are finite and positive, matching coefficient rows have 2..12 finite values with absolute value <=1. Sampling checks for floating-point overflow. The unchecked scalar `cst_shape` returns NaN for an empty coefficient list instead of panicking.

`CstFrame` carries a finite origin, nonzero finite physical chord vector and a nonzero finite orthogonal ordinate direction. Directions are numerically normalized; near-orthogonality is checked at 64 machine epsilons. This is not an exact frame certificate. `point(u,v)` maps dimensionless coordinates into that world plane. Physical chord length must be finite and representable. Frame axes may be authored in any 3D orientation, including a vertical chord.

The legacy inverse path previously normalized X but kept physical Y as coefficients and partitioned by global Y. Thus nonunit chord changed the recovered profile and translating both sides above Y=0 lost the lower surface. The corrected solver normalizes both coordinates, partitions relative to the inferred chord frame and returns that frame. Its default inference averages the extreme-X tip data and assumes both closed tips are present; it is a heuristic and is not a general chord detector. Use an authored frame when orientation or endpoints are ambiguous. All input stations must lie within that authored chord, up to a fixed floating-point endpoint allowance.

`cst_fit_in_frame` infers sides from the sign of the in-plane ordinate. `cst_fit_sides` instead accepts explicitly labeled side samples, supporting cambered profiles whose upper and lower ordinates have the same sign. Both paths share the existing 16..4096 total-site limit, order 1..11 and lambda in [0,1]. Tikhonov regularization is applied to dimensionless coordinates and is invariant to uniform world scaling. The normal-equation solver may refuse rank deficiency. Inverse coefficients are numerical fit results and may fall outside the forward sampler's coefficient bounds; checked sampling/construction of such a result can refuse.

`max_residual` measures the physical in-plane ordinate residual at fitted stations. `max_out_of_plane` separately reports source deviation outside the authored plane. `hausdorff_residual` is a one-way 3D data-site-to-discrete-sample distance using the existing 512 sampling intervals per side; it is neither symmetric nor a continuous Hausdorff certificate. Out-of-plane input is not silently asserted to be exactly planar. `CstFitResult::points` and `curve` reconstruct in the returned world frame.

The sampled `cst_airfoil` construction and inverse fits are approximate and do not certify continuous error. Neither construction path nor inverse fitting certifies simplicity, regularity, closure smoothness, B-rep ownership or aerodynamic performance. Independent tests cover polynomial profile equations, nonunit/translated inversion, vertical 3D placement, labeled cambered sides, world-scale regularization, explicit out-of-plane residuals, finite/invalid requests and unchanged limits. WASM, JSON, Rush, editor and STEP integration remain separate.


`cst_algebraic` is an alternative within the same construction capability, without sampled fitting. It accepts positive integer or half-integer `n1`, integer `n2`, and degree at most 25. The parameter substitution is `u=t^p`, with `p=1` for integer `n1` and `p=2` otherwise. Literal constant coefficient rows reduce exactly to shape order zero; a constant-row round-nose profile uses degree 3 and seven controls, while integer exponents `n1=n2=1` use degree 2 and five controls. Other rows retain their authored shape order. Generic positive exponents continue to use the sampled constructor.

For shape order `k`, let `m=p*n1` and `D=m+p*(n2+k)`. For `p=1`, shape coefficient `a_i` contributes `a_i*C(k,i)/C(D,m+i)` to ordinate control `m+i`. For `p=2`, set `q=n2+k-i`; the contribution to control `m+2*i+j` is `a_i*C(k,i)*C(q,j)*2^j/C(D,m+2*i+j)`, for `j=0..q`. This follows from `(1-t²)^q=(1-t)^q(1+t)^q` and direct conversion into Bernstein basis. Chord controls are `C(a,p)/C(D,p)`. Upper controls reverse the parameter; lower controls share the leading edge. The closed curve has two polynomial spans on [0,2], unit weights and at most 51 controls.

The immutable `AlgebraicCst` result retains the source profile, curve, chord, parameter power, consumed work and `max_error`. Every coefficient operation uses outward intervals. The maximum outward L1 control displacement bounds continuous nominal position error by nonnegative partition of unity; it also bounds Euclidean error. This concerns the mathematical curve defined by the returned binary64 controls. Machine evaluation roundoff, derivatives, external frame transforms, topology and previously fitted curves are excluded. Endpoints are copied exactly. No global certificate is minted.

One caller budget of 1..1,000,000 work units charges combinatorial steps, contributions, interval operations and output controls; exhaustion refuses the entire result. Existing degree, coordinate and profile bounds are unchanged. Independent exact rational control fixtures, exact symbolic coefficient identities, parameterization checks, constant-row reduction and budget boundary tests cover the direct path.

## Source section area and centroid

`cst_section_properties(&AlgebraicCst, max_work)` measures the original retained CST formula, not the rounded output curve. The result contains outward area and XY centroid intervals in chord units squared and chord units, plus consumed work. It uses the already admitted algebraic exponent/order subset. No sampled quadrature, tessellation or fitted-curve substitution occurs.

Admission conservatively requires each upper shape coefficient >= its lower counterpart, and at least one strict inequality. Since every interior Bernstein basis and the class factor are positive, this proves nonzero thickness and side order for the original source graph throughout (0,1). Profiles that need cancellation to prove ordering are refused. This is a formula-domain admission, not B-rep or general curve validity authority.

With `u=t^p`, normalized upper/lower source ordinates `U,L`, define `H=U-L` and `S=U+L`. The area is `chord²*p*integral(t^(p-1)*H)`. The centroid coordinates are `chord*integral(t^(2p-1)*H)/integral(t^(p-1)*H)` and `chord/2*integral(t^(p-1)*H*S)/integral(t^(p-1)*H)`. The common physical scale is canceled before division to avoid unnecessary chord-cubed underflow. Area must have a strictly positive lower interval endpoint; otherwise the operation refuses.

Differences and sums are formed with outward intervals before converting the source ordinates to Bernstein controls. Products use `B_i^n*B_j^m = C(n,i)*C(m,j)/C(n+m,i+j)*B_(i+j)^(n+m)`. Integrals use `integral(t^r*B_i^d)=product(i+1..i+r)/product(d+1..d+r+1)`. Here source degree <=25, product degree <=50 and r<=3. All binomial integer factors fit binary64 exactly; u64 intermediate binomial arithmetic stays in range. A single fresh operation budget charges every contribution, binomial step, interval primitive and integration-weight step. It does not reset between area and centroid calculations.

The intervals enclose the nominal original formula in its canonical chord plane. They do not enclose a later transformed frame, a solid volume, second moments, manufacturing variation or aerodynamic behavior. Very small area may be unrepresentable; arithmetic overflow, separation failure or budget exhaustion returns an error. Interval widths are observable and no requested-accuracy convergence is implied. Tests compare independent rational beta-integral area/centroid fixtures, including asymmetric same-side ordinates, degree-25 source shape, world scaling, exact work-boundary refusal and underflow.
